# Classification Rules

Use necessary conditions, M/N supporting evidence, veto conditions, and sector exceptions. Choose one primary investment type.

## A. Cigar-Butt Undervalued Asset

Necessary conditions:

- Valuation is significantly below the market or the company's own history.
- The thesis mainly comes from low price, not high business quality.

Supporting evidence, at least 3/5:

- P/E < 8
- P/B < 0.8
- EV/FCF < 8
- Market cap is near or below conservative liquidation value
- Dividend yield or buyback yield is significantly above market average

Veto conditions:

- Cash flow is deteriorating without signs of improvement
- Asset value is hard to realize
- High leverage may permanently dilute shareholders
- Management repeatedly damages shareholder value

Required answer: Is this a pricing error, or deteriorating fundamentals disguised as value?

## B. High-Quality Company at Reasonable Price

Necessary conditions:

- At least 4 of the past 5 years had positive FCF, or negative FCF is clearly explained by phase and economics.
- ROIC or ROE is above industry median and is not mainly caused by leverage or one-time factors.
- At least one verifiable moat exists: switching cost, pricing power, network effect, scale economy, brand trust, regulatory barrier, or data/workflow lock-in.
- Valuation does not require many years of high growth to be reasonable.

Supporting evidence, at least 5/8:

- 5-year average ROIC >= 15%, or materially above industry median
- 5-year average ROE >= 18% with controlled leverage
- 5-year FCF margin >= 10%, or materially above industry median
- Gross margin or operating margin is stable without persistent decline
- Cumulative FCF / cumulative net income >= 80%
- Net cash, or net debt / EBITDA < 2.5
- 5-year cumulative dilution < 10%, or offset by value-creating buybacks
- Current valuation is near or below the company's 5-year historical median and does not imply inferior return versus indexes

Veto conditions:

- Core moat is structurally weakening
- Long-term FCF conversion is below 60% without explanation
- SBC or dilution materially erodes shareholder return
- Valuation requires many years of high growth to be reasonable
- Leverage limits resilience under stress
- A high-probability, high-impact asymmetric destroyer already shows evidence of weakening the long-term profit pool

Sector exceptions:

- For SaaS, high-growth technology, biotech, and early platforms, do not mechanically require current FCF or ROE. Use retention, unit economics, gross margin, revenue quality, SBC, dilution, and path to cash generation instead.
- For AI applications and platform-dependent software, do not treat high revenue growth as high-quality SaaS unless inference cost, channel take rate, model/API dependency, CAC, and retention show durable unit economics.
- For financials, use ROE, P/B, asset quality, capital adequacy, and provisioning quality instead of ordinary-company FCF.

Required answer: Can the company maintain high capital returns, or is it only showing attractive mature-period numbers?

## C. Quantitative Mispricing With High Upside

Necessary conditions:

- Business quality is at least above average; it is not merely cheap.
- Market price reflects clear pessimism.
- Evidence suggests pessimism may be excessive rather than permanent impairment.
- Balance sheet can withstand at least 1-2 years of stress.

Supporting evidence, at least 5/8:

- FCF Yield >= risk-free rate + 4 percentage points
- FCF Yield >= benchmark index earnings yield or FCF Yield
- Forward P/E < 12, or EV/FCF < 10
- 5-year average ROIC >= 12%, or materially above industry median
- Past 3-year cumulative operating cash flow >= 80% of cumulative net income
- Net debt / EBITDA < 2.5, or net cash
- Core operating indicators did not keep deteriorating over the latest 2-4 quarters
- Market concern has an observable path to relief

Veto conditions:

- Undervaluation comes from permanent demand decline, technical substitution, or competitive deterioration
- Cheap-looking cash flow mainly comes from one-time investment cuts or working-capital compression
- Debt maturity pressure may force low-price financing, share issuance, or core asset sales
- Management repeatedly makes capital allocation decisions that harm shareholder return
- Inversion shows a high-probability, high-impact risk with visible evidence
- The thesis depends on a profit pool that can be made free, bundled, obsolete, or uneconomic by a player that does not need to profit from this product

Sector exceptions:

- For cyclicals, do not use peak-cycle earnings for Forward P/E or FCF Yield. Use mid-cycle earnings.
- For financials, prioritize P/B, ROE, asset quality, and capital adequacy over FCF Yield.
- For early high-growth companies, do not classify as this type unless unit economics and path to cash-flow breakeven are unusually clear.

Required answer: Is the market misunderstanding the business, or seeing deterioration earlier than the analyst?

## D. Value Trap

If any 3 apply, prioritize this classification:

- Valuation is cheap but revenue, margin, or cash flow keeps deteriorating
- Moat is weakening
- Industry demand is structurally declining
- Management repeatedly misallocates capital
- High leverage limits adaptation
- Technical substitution risk is visible
- Shareholder return mainly comes from financial engineering instead of operating quality

Required answer: Is the cheap valuation simply fair pricing for future decline?

## E. Overpriced Excellent Company

If any 3 apply, consider this classification:

- Company quality is high, but valuation is materially above history and peers
- Current price implies many years of high growth
- FCF Yield is materially below alternatives
- Stock is highly sensitive to rates, sentiment, or growth expectations
- Even if fundamentals remain good, 3-5 year shareholder return may be limited

Required answer: Is this a good company but not a good stock at the current price?

## Final Verdict Mapping

Final verdict must be one of:

- Reject
- Watchlist
- Medium-Term Revaluation Opportunity
- High-Quality Company at Reasonable Price
- High Conviction Candidate

High Conviction Candidate requires all:

- Data quality at least Medium
- Clear business model and strong moat
- Real and sustainable FCF, or equivalent sector-specific quality evidence
- Valuation provides meaningful safety margin
- Opportunity cost is clearly better than benchmark indexes
- No visible fire in inversion risks
- No high-probability, high-impact asymmetric destroyer with current evidence
- For AI applications and platform-dependent software, unit economics prove compute, channel, model/API, CAC, and retention risks are not consuming long-term excess profits
- User is inside the circle of competence
